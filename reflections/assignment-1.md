# Assignment 1 reflection

**What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?**

The brief asked for seven conceptual stages, and my first instinct was to
treat that as seven things to draw: a colour and a texture per named stage.
That's exactly the "slideshow" trap the brief warns against. The breakthrough
was realising the stages aren't states to render at all --- they're labels
overlaid on a handful of continuous functions of one progress number.
Dryness, sheen, scratch visibility, and the marbled cross-section each ramp up
and down smoothly across the whole 0--1 range; the seven names just mark
points on that curve. Once I saw it that way, "object continuity" stopped
being a constraint I had to keep checking against and became something the
architecture made automatic.

**What did this work change about who I want to be as a developer?**

I had several moments where a request was phrased reasonably but pointed at
something I shouldn't do --- reproducing a specific copyrighted video's frames
because it was "just for popular science," or inventing exact colours for a
reference page my tools genuinely couldn't inspect. It would have been easy to
either flatly refuse and stall the work, or to quietly comply and hope it was
fine. What I want to keep doing is the third option: name the actual
constraint out loud, and then still find and ship something legitimate that
serves the same underlying goal --- an original procedural texture instead of
a copied one, an original design in the same idiom instead of a fabricated
"match." Good judgement isn't just knowing where the line is; it's still
delivering something real once you've found it.
